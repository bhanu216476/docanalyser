## 1. Overview & Architecture

BM25 (Best Matching 25) is a probabilistic ranking function used for sparse lexical (keyword-based) search. While dense vector retrieval excels at capturing semantic intent and fuzzy meaning, it can miss exact terms, serial numbers, codes, or rare specialized terminology. BM25 provides exact keyword matching grounded in information retrieval theory.

In DocAnalyser, BM25 retrieval operates as an independent sparse retriever alongside dense retrieval:

```text
                       User Query
                           │
                 ┌─────────┴─────────┐
                 ▼                   ▼
          Dense Retrieval       BM25 Retrieval
          (DenseRetriever)      (BM25Retriever)
                 │                   │
                 ▼                   ▼
           Dense Results        BM25 Results
```

Both retrievers implement the structural `Retriever` protocol (`app.retrieval.retriever.Retriever`) and produce standardized, typed `RetrievalResult` objects.

## 2. Core Concepts

### Term Frequency (TF) and Saturation

Term Frequency measures how many times a query term appears in a document:

$$\text{TF}(q, D) = \text{frequency of term } q \text{ in document } D$$

In basic TF schemes, a document mentioning a word 10 times is considered 10 times more relevant than one mentioning it once. However, relevance does not scale linearly. In BM25, the term frequency is passed through a **saturation function**:

$$\frac{\text{TF} \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{\text{TF} + k_1}$$

As $\text{TF} \to \infty$, the fraction approaches $k_1 + 1$. This prevents long, repetitive documents from dominating search results.

---

### Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)

Inverse Document Frequency measures how rare or informative a query term is across the entire corpus of documents:

$$\text{IDF}(q) = \text{importance of term } q \text{ based on rarity}$$

Common words appearing across almost all documents contribute very little information, whereas rare terms contribute high discriminative value.

To ensure stability and prevent negative scores for terms present in over half the corpus, we implement the smoothed Robertson-Spärck Jones formulation:

$$\text{IDF}(q) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{N - \text{df}(q) + 0.5}{\text{df}(q) + 0.5}\right)$$

Where:
- $N$: Total number of chunks in the corpus.
- $\text{df}(q)$: Number of chunks containing query term $q$.
- Because $\frac{N - \text{df}(q) + 0.5}{\text{df}(q) + 0.5} \ge 0$, the argument to the logarithm is always $\ge 1$, guaranteeing $\text{IDF}(q) \ge 0$ for all terms.

---

### Document Length Normalization

Without length normalization, longer documents would naturally accumulate higher scores simply by having more words. BM25 normalizes term frequency according to document length relative to the collection average:

$$\frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}$$

Where:
- $|D|$: Length of document $D$ (in tokens).
- $\text{avgdl}$: Average document length across all documents in the corpus ($\frac{1}{N}\sum_{d} |d|$).

The normalization penalty applied to the TF saturation denominator is:

$$\text{len\_norm} = 1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}$$

- When $|D| = \text{avgdl}$, $\text{len\_norm} = 1.0$.
- When $|D| > \text{avgdl}$, $\text{len\_norm} > 1.0$, which increases the denominator and dampens the score.
- When $|D| < \text{avgdl}$, $\text{len\_norm} < 1.0$, which boosts the score of concise chunks.

---

## 3. The Full BM25 Formula

Combining TF saturation, smoothed IDF, and length normalization:

$$\text{BM25}(D, Q) = \sum_{q_i \in Q} \text{IDF}(q_i) \cdot \frac{\text{TF}(q_i, D) \cdot (k_1 + 1)}{\text{TF}(q_i, D) + k_1 \cdot \left(1 - b + b \cdot \frac{|D|}{\text{avgdl}}\right)}$$

### Parameters

- **$k_1$ (Term Frequency Saturation)**:
  - Controls how quickly the score saturates with repeated term occurrences.
  - Default: $k_1 = 1.2$.
  - When $k_1 = 0$, TF is ignored completely (binary occurrence).
  - As $k_1 \to \infty$, score increases roughly linearly with TF.
- **$b$ (Length Normalization)**:
  - Controls the strength of document length normalization.
  - Default: $b = 0.75$.
  - When $b = 0$: Length normalization is completely disabled ($\text{len\_norm} = 1.0$). Long and short documents are treated identically.
  - When $b = 1$: Full length normalization is enforced; term frequency is scaled strictly relative to document length.

---

## 4. Tokenization Pipeline

Both indexed document chunks and incoming user queries are processed with the identical deterministic tokenization pipeline in `app.retrieval.tokenizer`:

1. Case normalization to lowercase.
2. Boundary-aware word extraction: `re.findall(r"\b\w+\b", text.lower())`.
3. Punctuation stripping and repeated whitespace collapsing.
4. Preserves numbers and alphanumeric identifiers.
5. No aggressive or lossy stemming.

## 5. Worked Numerical Example

Consider a small toy corpus with $N = 3$ documents:

- **Chunk A**: *"Employees receive casual leave every year."*
- **Chunk B**: *"Employees receive annual leave and sick leave."*
- **Chunk C**: *"Office parking policy and vehicle regulations."*

### Token Counts & Lengths
- Tokens in Chunk A: `["employees", "receive", "casual", "leave", "every", "year"]` $\to |D_A| = 6$
- Tokens in Chunk B: `["employees", "receive", "annual", "leave", "and", "sick", "leave"]` $\to |D_B| = 7$
- Tokens in Chunk C: `["office", "parking", "policy", "and", "vehicle", "regulations"]` $\to |D_C| = 6$
- Total tokens: $6 + 7 + 6 = 19$
- Average document length: $\text{avgdl} = 19 / 3 \approx 6.3333$

### Query
**Query**: `"casual leave"` $\to$ tokens: `["casual", "leave"]`

### IDF Calculation ($N = 3$)
- `"casual"` appears only in Chunk A $\to \text{df} = 1$:
  $$\text{IDF}(\text{"casual"}) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{3 - 1 + 0.5}{1 + 0.5}\right) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{2.5}{1.5}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{8}{3}\right) \approx 0.980829$$
- `"leave"` appears in Chunk A and Chunk B $\to \text{df} = 2$:
  $$\text{IDF}(\text{"leave"}) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{3 - 2 + 0.5}{2 + 0.5}\right) = \ln\left(1 + \frac{1.5}{2.5}\right) = \ln\left(\frac{8}{5}\right) \approx 0.470004$$

### Scoring ($k_1 = 1.2$, $b = 0.75$)

#### Chunk A
- $|D_A| = 6 \implies \frac{|D_A|}{\text{avgdl}} = \frac{6}{19/3} = \frac{18}{19} \approx 0.947368$
- $\text{len\_norm}_A = 1 - 0.75 + 0.75 \times \frac{18}{19} = \frac{73}{76} \approx 0.960526$
- Term `"casual"`: $\text{TF} = 1$
  $$\text{TF\_comp}_A(\text{"casual"}) = \frac{1 \times 2.2}{1 + 1.2 \times 0.960526} = \frac{2.2}{2.152632} \approx 1.022005$$
  $$\text{Score}_A(\text{"casual"}) = 0.980829 \times 1.022005 \approx 1.002412$$
- Term `"leave"`: $\text{TF} = 1$
  $$\text{TF\_comp}_A(\text{"leave"}) = \frac{2.2}{2.152632} \approx 1.022005$$
  $$\text{Score}_A(\text{"leave"}) = 0.470004 \times 1.022005 \approx 0.480346$$
- **Total BM25 Score for Chunk A**: $1.002412 + 0.480346 \approx \mathbf{1.482758}$

#### Chunk B
- $|D_B| = 7 \implies \frac{|D_B|}{\text{avgdl}} = \frac{7}{19/3} = \frac{21}{19} \approx 1.105263$
- $\text{len\_norm}_B = 1 - 0.75 + 0.75 \times \frac{21}{19} = \frac{82}{76} \approx 1.078947$
- Term `"casual"`: $\text{TF} = 0 \implies 0.0$
- Term `"leave"`: $\text{TF} = 2$
  $$\text{TF\_comp}_B(\text{"leave"}) = \frac{2 \times 2.2}{2 + 1.2 \times 1.078947} = \frac{4.4}{3.294737} \approx 1.335463$$
  $$\text{Score}_B(\text{"leave"}) = 0.470004 \times 1.335463 \approx 0.627673$$
- **Total BM25 Score for Chunk B**: $\mathbf{0.627673}$

#### Chunk C
- Neither `"casual"` nor `"leave"` occurs in Chunk C.
- **Total BM25 Score for Chunk C**: $\mathbf{0.0}$

### Final Ranked Output
1. **Rank 1**: Chunk A — Score: `1.4828` (matches rare term "casual" and "leave")
2. **Rank 2**: Chunk B — Score: `0.6277` (matches "leave" twice, penalized by length and missing "casual")
3. **Chunk C**: Excluded (Score = 0.0)

## 6. Deterministic Tie-Breaking & Filtering

- **Metadata Pre-Filtering**: When metadata filters (`document_id`, `file_type`, `source`, `section`) are specified, candidate chunks are filtered **before** ranking. BM25 is evaluated only over matching chunks.
- **Deterministic Tie-Breaking**: Chunks with identical BM25 scores are ordered using `chunk_id` as the secondary sort key: `(-score, chunk_id)`.
